# M0 laptop thermal soak, 16 October 2026

The laptop completed 3,600.0004 seconds of simultaneous persistent stem
delivery, animated Wayland/OpenGL UI and PipeWire output. The 4.095-second hop
p99 target failed: **9.769 seconds p99, 11.795 seconds maximum and 35/1,510
continuous hops above target (2.32%)**. All eleven cancellation/seek checks
passed, with no stale chunks or output mismatch. The independent silent audio
probe recorded zero observed faults. M0 remains open; M1 has not started.

This follows the [10.813-second stall investigation](stall-trace-2026-10-16.md).
The separator binary, model, blocking fence wait, child-local passive OpenMP
policy and fixed-grid overlap-add are unchanged. This change adds the hour-long
benchmark, memory/pressure/power-state observations and checks that the UI and
audio really cover the workload. It introduces no inference workaround.

The machine is the ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 9, i7-1185G7/Iris Xe, 16 GB RAM,
running Arch Linux, kernel `7.2.10-hardened1-1-hardened`, Mesa 26.2.4-arch1.1,
Qt 6.12.0, PipeWire 1.6.9 and NumPy 2.5.4. AC was online. A temporary
`powerprofilesctl launch --profile performance` hold covered each performance
trial; firmware profile and CPU EPP were sampled during the run. The CPU
governor reported `powersave`, which does not identify the whole power policy.
No build, compression or full-trace analysis ran during the final workload.
Other desktop activity was not isolated. These were sequential trials on an
already exercised laptop, without a controlled cool-down.

The fixture contains 2,076,225 frames of deterministic synthetic stereo
44.1 kHz PCM16 audio, 47.080 seconds, repeated through one loaded backend for
the hour. Its SHA-256 is
`840561b2f6c6cd26ab70b0e2adef629f3eee09878333b1e41f901972504546c4`.
Whole-source normalization therefore differs from the earlier longer
fixtures. Output comparisons use identical windows within this fixture. The
7.8-second inference window and 5.85-second output hop retain their prior
definitions; the target is 70% of one hop. Native/system observations were
requested every 0.5 seconds. Mesa GPU tracing was disabled.

Three long attempts are retained separately. The first completed an hour but
lost UI rendering coverage. The second stopped when an overly strict exposure
check rejected a brief transition. Only the final trial satisfies the requested
full audio/UI workload.

The earlier 10.813-second graph stall remains associated with a long period at
100 MHz actual GPU frequency despite a 1350 MHz request, with PL1 asserted.
The initial hour reproduced four similar delays without Mesa GPU tracing.
The guarded partial trial reproduced the pattern more often: 24 of its 27
all-delivered misses had a 100 MHz median actual clock, and all 27 had a
1350 MHz median requested clock. Render accounting continued advancing,
runtime suspension did not increase, and identical-window good requests took
1.796–3.573 seconds. This is a recurring low-frequency phase while the GT
remains awake. PL1 also occurs on good work; these observations do not identify
the firmware/platform-power versus driver trigger.

Recorded package temperatures ranged from 65 to 100 °C; the separate TCPU
sensor ranged from 63.05 to 96.05 °C. CPU0 core/package throttle counters rose
by 12,241/55,669. Sibling counters are not added as independent events. High
temperatures and throttle flags also occur during passing requests.

The final hour strengthens that finding. Thirty-two of its 35 misses had a
100 MHz median actual clock, while all 35 had a 1350 MHz median requested
clock. PL1 was asserted in 73–100% of each miss's snapshots. Render accounting
covered 53–79% of the strict sampled interior, RC6 increased by only 0–202 ms,
and device runtime-suspended time did not increase. These measurements describe
activity/accounting and residency, not useful shader arithmetic. Snapshot
fractions are not weighted by elapsed time.

The worst request, 1473, took 11.795 seconds including 11.706 seconds in the
graph, 144.726 ms native CPU time and 0.109 ms queue delay, with no graph rebuild.
Identical-window requests 1464 and 1482 took 1.892 and 2.353 seconds, with
identical output hashes. Its strict interior shows no memory-pressure,
reclaim, swap or native-fault increase. The package begins at 100 °C and cools;
three snapshots assert the GPU thermal flag. Other misses include roughly
48–50 ms of memory PSI and reclaim activity. That evidence does not support a
common multi-second memory-pressure explanation, but does not exclude every
driver/GEM memory path. The bounded accessible kernel graphics journal has no
matching entries; unreadable error-state data and journal coverage limit that
negative evidence.

The first miss arrived within 94 seconds, so the failure is not confined to
the end of the soak. All seventy identical-window good comparisons matched
their slow counterparts' output hashes. The largest sample gap was 722.477 ms;
capture time had a 6.702 ms median and 207.761 ms maximum. The trace preserves
unsampled request edges and read intervals crossing a boundary. RAPL energy
reads were denied, and `sched_schedstats` was disabled. Native fdinfo reads
had two errors; the supervisor summary had 7,192 fdinfo errors. Missing fields
are omitted and counted, never substituted with zero. The supervisor has no
full raw trace, so its resource summary cannot be independently reaggregated.

Cancellation checks ran every five minutes, after at least 150 ms following a
matching supervisor `started` event. The harness revalidated generation and
request identity immediately before cancellation. It does not observe the
hardware submission timestamp. Each check rejected a held old chunk, delivered
zero chunks during the canceled generation, then sought alternately forward
and backward to off-grid frames. All eleven trimmed results matched their
continuous-grid reference exactly, with maximum absolute difference zero.
Nine checks held a chunk from the generation being canceled; two held an
already obsolete chunk from the preceding source-loop generation. Thus nine
checks exercise invalidation of a current held chunk, while all eleven check
active-generation cancellation and replacement.

Cancellation invalidates output immediately; it does not preempt the active
Vulkan graph. Replacement can also need the predecessor window for overlap-add.
The scheduled checks do not establish the longest possible drain during every
stall. Hard shutdown with a request still in flight took 157.077 ms. The worker
loaded one backend, discarded eleven superseded requests, reached a ready-queue
high-water mark of one against a limit of two, and dropped no telemetry. There
were 1,501 same-grid hash checks, eleven trimmed seek checks and 156 full source
loops.

The audio stream covered the entire 3,600.0004-second workload intersection.
It ran for 3,601.690 seconds at negotiated 48 kHz stereo with a fixed 256-frame
quantum, producing 675,295 callbacks and 172,875,520 frames. Callback p99.9 was
at most 23 microseconds; maximum execution was 515.415 microseconds. Deadline
misses, missing/invalid buffers, truncated frames, observed graph-position
discontinuities and observed clock xrun-recovery flags were all zero. These
are callback and reported-clock measurements. The signal was silence; stems
were not routed into PipeWire, and neither end-to-end speaker latency nor a
complete graph xrun count was measured.

The final UI produced 213,072 GUI-thread `frameSwapped` notifications. All sixty
complete minute bins contained frames; the maximum notification gap was
295.767 ms. One sampled heartbeat reported `exposed=false`, then recovered at
the next sampled timestamp 0.990 seconds later. Qt's
[exposure flag](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6.12/qwindow.html#isExposed) describes the
window-system exposed state. This is a sampled exposure
span, not the exact time the window was invisible. The heartbeat/frame gate
passed with a 30-second inactivity/exposure budget and an initialized scene
graph. These observations establish sustained M0 animation work, not smooth
physical presentation or the later full-deck UI workload.

The first hour had 23 empty complete UI minute bins and a 965.997-second maximum
notification gap despite an alive process and a successful final render. Its
raw `status: ok` is preserved alongside a failed coverage qualification. The
guarded partial trial had no empty complete minute bins and a 199.384 ms maximum
gap, but aborted on one instantaneous false exposure flag. Its raw
`status: error` remains unchanged. The corrected gate records exposure
transitions and their spans, permits brief transitions, and rejects stale
heartbeats, stopped frames, an uninitialized scene graph or an exposure loss
over 30 seconds. A recovered over-budget span still fails.

The benchmark-only KWin wrapper holds a live desktop idle inhibitor and keeps
only its verified child PID/app-ID window above other windows on all desktops.
It saves and restores that window's state, unloads its temporary script and
releases the inhibitor. The final lifecycle record confirms cleanup with no
errors. The performance hold released, the prior `power-saver` profile returned
with no holds, and the generated fixture was removed. No global desktop,
driver or power configuration was changed.

The native process peaked at 281,696 kB RSS/HWM with zero observed swap; the
supervisor peaked at 150,416 kB RSS and 164,860 kB HWM with zero observed swap.
Observed process CPU time was 184.10 seconds native and 110.07 seconds
supervisor between successful first/last samples. These figures exclude GPU
allocations and must not be added to DRM client maxima as a shared-memory peak.
The sampler now streams raw rows and retains aggregate counters instead of
every snapshot. PCM references, source reads and the ready queue stay bounded;
the soak retains per-hop measurement metadata for its finite run.

No model weights, generated audio or binaries are committed. Reproduction

Validation included the application build, all three CTest targets (including
sixteen worker cases), offscreen rendering/progress output and its unwritable
path error, sampler parsing/aggregation checks, and focused live/final UI-gate
checks. The owned-window guard passed a real 20-second cleanup test. Host GPU
short runs passed before each long attempt. Independent event/trace audits
recomputed percentiles, source hashes, frame continuity, controls and overlap.

M0 still needs a controlled sustained power-policy comparison and a verified
remedy for the recurring low-clock phase before claiming reliable delivery.
Stem routing, musical quality/seam checks, physical audio latency and full-deck
load remain outside this synthetic soak. M1 has not started.
