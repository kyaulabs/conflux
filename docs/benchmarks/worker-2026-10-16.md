# Persistent stem worker, 16 October 2026

The persistent worker passes the tested delivery, seek and cancellation checks,
but this HTDemucs configuration misses the live processing headroom target on
the primary laptop. M0 remains open and M1 has not started. Keep Vulkan as an
experimental separator backend while investigating latency and musical quality;
these measurements do not justify admitting live stems into the audio engine.

The [worker and reproduction instructions](../../tools/stems/README.md) describe
the isolated Python/NumPy supervisor and persistent C++20 child. One model and
graph stay loaded across ordinary controls. Generation changes immediately
invalidate queued and held output. An active Vulkan request finishes before
replacement work can start; cancellation does not preempt GPU computation.
Timeout and hard shutdown can terminate the child.

The worker uses normalized stereo 44.1 kHz PCM16 files, 343,980-frame windows
(7.8 s), 257,985-frame hops (5.85 s) and normalized triangular overlap-add.
The first hop requires source audio through 7.8 s, including 1.95 s beyond its
end. Whole-source hashing and mono sample mean/std preparation happen first in
bounded passes, then are cached across seeks. This file-based experiment does
not accept an incoming live audio stream. Seeks recompute the preceding window
on the fixed source grid and trim output to the requested source frame.

Tests ran sequentially on the ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 9, i7-1185G7, 16 GB RAM
and Iris Xe TGL GT2, under the normal Wayland desktop. Vulkan reported device
API 1.4.354 and Mesa 26.2.4-arch1.1. The separator requested four backend threads.
Each run kept a small animated Qt 6.12 window and a silent PipeWire 1.6.9 output
stream active throughout the worker scenarios. Qt actually used OpenGL or
Vulkan as requested. Audio negotiated 48 kHz stereo and 256-frame quanta.
No global audio or power settings changed. AC was online and the CPU governor
was `powersave` at both endpoint snapshots. CPU package temperature changed
42→66 °C for OpenGL and 50→49 °C for Vulkan; these snapshots cannot establish
whether throttling occurred between them. Other desktop activity and driver
caches were not isolated or reset.

The native adapter used audio.cpp v0.9.2 at
`0097d365a00b52a6991ad7618f0a04e8d2935e47`, built locally with GCC 16.2.1,
Release `-O3 -DNDEBUG`, Vulkan, OpenMP and native CPU support. Shaders used
glslc 2026.4 / Vulkan 1.4.363. Debug result checks and Vulkan validation were
off. [Build metadata](data/worker/build.json) records binary, archive, header
and model hashes. The F16 model is the same 84,059,168-byte artifact as the
[earlier offline benchmark](stems-2026-10-16.md), which also records runtime
and weight license sources. The temporary Khronos SPIR-V header is MIT-licensed.
The previous upstream binary used GCC 13.3.0; its shader compiler is unknown.
The two builds' timings are not an isolated adapter-overhead comparison.

Both sustained runs used the same deterministic, annotated synthetic fixture:
15,491,445 source frames, or 351.280 s. Each delivered 60 full hops plus a
12,345-frame tail, contiguously and with the exact source frame count. The
fixture contains bass, kick, noise hats, changing tones, asymmetric transients
and a tempo change. It is permitted timing input, not a music-quality corpus.

Hop service measures source-window read/conversion, temporary-file IPC, native
transforms, graph execution, reconstruction, output read and overlap-add. It
excludes waiting on a full ready queue and the initial whole-source preparation.
First-ready latency includes model hashing/loading and source preparation;
source preparation alone took 1.021 s and 0.951 s respectively. Delivered time
also includes benchmark polling and sample hashing. Only PCM WAV decoding was
exercised. RTF divides total continuous-run time by source duration.

The initial target is p99 below 70% of a hop, or 4.095 s. OpenGL exceeded that
limit on 16/61 hops and Vulkan on 21/61; each had two hops above 5.85 s. Both
miss the target despite aggregate processing faster than playback. Percentiles
use nearest rank; with 61 samples, p99 is the observed maximum, not a tail
latency guarantee. Maximum native graph time was 6.003 s and 6.190 s. Graph
time includes uploads, backend computation, synchronization, readback and driver
waits. Graph rebuild time was zero throughout these delivered hops.

An earlier seven-hop pilot also remains in the [raw data](data/worker/pilot/).
First-ready latency was 11.048 s and maximum hop service was 13.357 s, including
a 13.015 s graph on the third window. The pilot's resource-summary code failed
on a DRM field changing from raw `0` to a quantity. No valid resource or final
UI/audio summary survived that run. The aggregator was fixed and regression
checked before the sustained runs. Upstream lazily compiles Vulkan pipelines,
but that does not explain every later spike. Neither compilation, throttling
nor telemetry overhead has been established as the cause.

Each paced test began when its first hop arrived, then consumed five more at
5.85 s intervals with at most two hops queued. Both recorded zero late-ready
handoffs across 29.25 s. Six delivered hops contain 35.1 s of material; the
final hop was not played through. The OpenGL raw JSON's older
`simulated_playback_seconds` field means delivered material, corrected to
`delivered_audio_seconds` in the current tool. This consumer tests scheduling
readiness while PipeWire independently renders silence. It does not establish
continuous stem playback or resampling into the 48 kHz audio graph.

Both seek outputs matched their continuous-grid reference exactly, starting at
frames 516,107 and 258,318. Shape and numeric parity are assertions, not just
recorded diagnostics. Latest-control-wins bursts, source identity replacement,
held-result rejection and queue cancellation passed. No stale or post-cancel
chunk was delivered. Each run loaded the native backend once, discarded six
superseded requests, reached a ready-queue high-water mark of two and closed
the child successfully. Fast control acknowledgement does not imply fast
replacement audio; residual work and seek preroll account for separate waits.

Process status and DRM fdinfo were sampled every 100 ms. DRM clients are
deduplicated by device/client ID; original fields and units are retained.
The total/resident maxima overlap and can occur at different times. They must
not be added to each other or RSS as a total GPU/shared-memory peak. Supervisor
memory includes four reference hops, telemetry and benchmark sampling. Some
individual fdinfo reads failed; counts are retained, but their cause was not
diagnosed. CPU time spans first through last successful sample, excluding
earlier process work.

The concurrent silent audio probes ran for 248.469 s and 295.871 s, with
46,585 and 55,470 callbacks. Callback p99.9 upper bounds were 28 and 30 µs;
maxima were 65.860 and 67.367 µs. Both reported zero callback deadline misses,
missing/invalid buffers, truncated frames, observed graph-position
discontinuities and observed clock recovery flags. These are probe counters,
not physical output latency or a complete graph xrun count. Qt completed
14,830 and 17,655 frame swaps; frame counts do not measure interaction latency
or worst frame stalls. The workload has no decks, cue, recording or effects.

Both runs had recombination RMS error 0.002098 relative to full scale and
maximum adjacent-hop stem sample step 0.079458. These are numerical diagnostics,
not audible-seam or separation-quality judgments. The 15 supervisor tests cover
OLA against a scalar oracle, short/EOF cases, seeks, source changes, stale
results, queue bounds, cancellation, runtime faults and protocol timeouts
without requiring weights or a GPU. Application build, CTest and offscreen QML
checks also pass. CI tests this model-free path; it does not benchmark Vulkan.

[OpenGL results](data/worker/opengl/result.json),
[Vulkan results](data/worker/vulkan/result.json) and their adjacent event logs
retain timings, identities, controls, conditions and UI/audio reports. No
weights, generated audio or third-party binaries are committed. No model/API
charges or Apollo processing were used. The next M0 work is to investigate the
observed graph/delivery spikes and compare a chunk/model alternative against
annotated music and listening tests. Live headroom and musical quality remain
open; full-workload thermal acceptance also remains pending. M1 has not started.
